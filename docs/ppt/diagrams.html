Slide 2 — Proposed Solution / Approach

Proposed solution — five bullets
  • Ice Forecast Correction: The AI learns to correct the official sea-ice forecast, beating the standard baseline by 18.5% at 1 day and 30.6% at 7 days.
  • Iceberg Drift: A proven physics model computes the current drift velocity and regime for each of 33 tracked icebergs (USNIC, 27 Aug 2026), 15 of them inside our shipping corridor. Projecting a future track over time is designed, not built.
  • Safe Routing: The router plans the safest path on real satellite ice and models Antarctic protected areas by the legal regime that applies to each — none currently restrict the ship's transit, only shore operations. Live re-planning is designed, not built.
  • Ashore and Aboard: Heavy work runs ashore; the ship gets one small daily update over Iridium and runs the full router on board with no internet.
  • Captain Decides: The system advises and shows what changed and why. Naming which fallback grade it is using is designed (ADR-022), not built yet. The captain always makes the call.
Innovation and uniqueness — five cards
  • Correct, Not Replace — The AI corrects the official forecast; if it fails, the forecast stands.
  • Coastal Blind Spot — Satellite data marks blocked coast as open water; we found it and fixed it.
  • Built for Iridium — A 76 KB pack and a 796-byte route fit the ship's link.
  • Safety Grades, Designed — Three degradation grades are specified (ADR-022: forecast-grade → CMEMS-direct → climatology-grade); not wired into the demo yet.
  • Self-Check Is Specified, Not Automated — Scoring yesterday's forecast against today's image is designed; only the daily CMEMS harvest actually runs on a schedule today.

Slide 3 — Technical Approach

Tech stack and the six implementation steps

Tech Stack: Python, PyTorch, xarray, NetCDF for data and AI; PolarRoute, meshiphi for routing; GitHub Actions, FastAPI, MapLibre for automation, API and maps.

  1. Harvest Satellite Data — A daily harvest on GitHub Actions pulls real satellite ice data automatically, because missed days can never be back-filled.
  2. Audit and Fix — We checked 1,096 daily files with zero failures, found the coastal blind spot, and made the coast read heavier, not lighter.
  3. Train the Correction — PyTorch learns a correction to the official forecast; test data is split by date and scored exactly once.
  4. Track Icebergs — A published physics model drifts each iceberg; 23 of 23 tests reproduce the paper's own numbers.
  5. Compute the Route — PolarRoute and meshiphi find the safest path on real ice: 8.6 days steaming, Cape Town to Bharati.
  6. Pack and Ship Aboard — A full pack is 76 KB compressed and the daily update is budgeted at 50 KB, so it fits the ship's link.

Slide 4 — Feasibility and Viability

Feasibility
  • Proven Model: Beats the standard baseline by 18.5% at 1 day and 30.6% at 7 days on real held-out satellite data.
  • Real Pipeline: 1,096 daily satellite files processed with zero failures, harvested automatically every day.
  • Reused Engines: PolarRoute and meshiphi from the British Antarctic Survey already compute our 8.6-day route.
Viability
  • Fits the Link: A full data pack is 76 KB and a route summary is 796 bytes, well inside Iridium's 704 kbps.
  • Small Archive: Compression cuts each daily file from 42.6 MB to 5.0 MB, 8.4x smaller.
  • Open Source: Every tool in the stack is open source and the satellite data is public, with nothing to license.
Practical Implementation
  • Runs Offline: The ship runs the full router locally with no internet; only a small daily update comes over Iridium.
  • Fails Safe: The model outputs a correction to the official forecast, so if it fails the crew still has the official forecast, not nothing.
  • Already Running: The daily data harvest started early and runs automatically, because missed days can never be back-filled.
Challenges → Strategies
  • Connectivity: South of 70 degrees South the only link is Iridium, about 704 kbps at best.  →  Heavy work stays ashore; the ship gets one small daily update and runs the full router offline.
  • Moving Ice: Ice can shift faster than any forecast can follow.  →  The system re-checks every day against the latest satellite image and re-plans the route.
  • Blind Spots: Satellite data marks blocked coast as open water on 43.2% of days in the approach to Bharati.  →  We flag those pixels so the coast reads heavier, not lighter, and the router turns more cautious, never less.
  • Fuel Data: No fuel-burn figure exists yet for this exact hull.  →  We rank routes by steaming time and report fuel relatively or not at all.
  • Small Ice: Growlers, small chunks of ice, are invisible from orbit.  →  We never claim to spot them; radar and the lookout own that job, and the system says so.

Slide 5 — Impact and Benefits

Who it helps — five audiences
  • Ship's Crew: Sees where the ice will be, which icebergs are near, and why the route changed, with the captain always in command.
  • NCPOR: Plans the Cape Town to Bharati resupply on real ice forecasts and knows in advance when the Bharati approach is closed.
  • Scientists: Supplies and teams reach Bharati and Maitri on time, and our coastal data audit exposes a flaw in the satellite record they rely on.
  • The Nation: India gets a home-built decision tool for its Antarctic programme, built on open data and open code.
  • The Environment: The router stays inside the protected-area boundaries that India co-proposed, and safer routes cut the risk of grounding or spills.
Social, Economic, Environmental
  • Social: Crew safety comes first: the system explains what changed and why, and never asks the captain to trust a black box.
  • Economic: Fewer stuck days and smarter routes save costly ship time on a chartered vessel.
  • Environmental: Cautious routing through heavy ice lowers the risk of hull damage and spills in Antarctic waters.
