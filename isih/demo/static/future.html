Baymax Mode SIMULATED

§25 — a watch-worn assistant for the officer on watch.

This is not a medical device and does not give medical advice. It is a fatigue-and-workload aid of the kind bridge resource management already uses, and the values below are a fixed demonstration sequence — not readings from any person.

What it would say

    To become real this needs: a watch or wearable exposing heart-rate variability and SpO₂, a sleep model validated against watchkeeping rosters, and — because it touches crew health data — a consent and data-protection position that does not exist yet. Any of those missing is why it is on this page and not on the bridge console.

    Master's assistant NOT A LANGUAGE MODEL

    §26 — a locally run assistant, so the ship keeps it when the link drops.

    The replies below are composed from the live decision object — the same gates, health and corridors the bridge console serves. They are templated, not generated. No model runs here, locally or otherwise. What this demonstrates is the interaction: asking the system a question in words and getting an answer that cites its own evidence.

    To become real this needs a small instruction-tuned model that runs on the bridge laptop without a GPU, and a retrieval layer over the decision object and the research documents. The honest reason to run it locally is the same reason the rest of the system is offline: south of about 70°S the link is Iridium or nothing.

    Why these are separated

    The bridge console shows only measured or computed values, each with its source. Mixing a simulated health monitor into that screen would make every real number on it answerable to the question "so what else here is fake?".

    Keeping the simulation on its own page is the cheapest way to keep the console's claims intact — and it is what §25 asks for: integrated and smooth, but never represented as real operational or medical capability.
